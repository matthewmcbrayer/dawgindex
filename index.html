What is this?

We grade every Power 4 game on one question: did the team show up?

  1. Not wins. Expectations. Did they beat the Vegas line, finish drives, pull away with a lead, answer back?
  2. Big games count more. Road games, night games, packed stadiums, ranked opponents.
  3. Every game gets a grade, measured against every Power 4 performance since 2019 (5,900 of them). 0 = played exactly like Vegas expected · +5 = better than 84% of them · +10 = better than 96% · below zero = worse than expected
  4. Every team gets a DAWG grade: the average of its game grades. +3 or better = DAWG · −3 or worse = SOFT

How the Grade Works

Big Games

Each game gets a stage score, the same for both teams except where noted: ranked opponent +15 (top 10 +25), ranked vs. ranked +10, rivalry +20 (traditional rivalries only, like the Iron Bowl, Red River or The Game), national TV (ABC, CBS, NBC, FOX, ESPN) +10, primetime on national TV +10 more, bowl, playoff or title game +30, neutral-site showcase +20, non-conference Power 4 matchup +10, packed stadium (90%+ of its biggest crowd, night crowds count 1.25×) +10, and an opponent that won 10+ games the season before +10. 25 or more is a big game, so it takes at least two of those. Against a team outside the Power 4, it only counts if that team is ranked or it's a bowl, playoff or title game: a night game on TV against an unranked Group of 5 or FCS team isn't a test, no matter how loud it gets (rule added Oct. 4, 2026). Contenders in big conferences play more of them, and that's intended.

Garbage time is out

Only competitive plays count. A play is garbage time when the lead before it is more than 38 in the 2nd quarter, 28 in the 3rd or 22 in the 4th. If the lead shrinks back under the line, plays count again, so a collapse still shows up.

vs spread

Did they beat the expectation set before kickoff? Home field is already priced in.

margin (no garbage time) + closing line

Finishing

Did the scoreboard match the yardage? Out-gaining a team and losing is a finishing gap.

margin − the margin those yards usually produce

Knife twist

Once up 9 or more, does the lead grow? Net points from that moment until garbage time, compared with what's normal for that matchup.

Cash-in

Points on the very next drive after the defense gets a stop or a takeaway, compared with what's normal for a team favored by that much. The national average is about 45% of those drives scoring.

Answers

When the opponent scores to make it a one-score game, how often does the next drive score?

The game grade

Each part is scored by how rare it is compared with every Power 4 game since 2019, so there's no ceiling: a 30-point cover counts for more than a 14-point one. Answers rests on a handful of drives, so it counts half as much as the other four. A game needs at least three of the five parts. The average is scaled so about two-thirds of games land between −5 and +5.

average of the five parts × setting

Setting

Bigger stages magnify the grade, good or bad: road +10%, night +5%, full road stadium (90%+, night crowds count 1.25×) +10%, ranked opponent +10% (top 10 +20%), postseason +10%. A good grade against a Group of 5 team shrinks 15%, against an FCS team 40%; a bad grade against them is never softened.

Season DAWG and big-stage drop

A season's DAWG is the average of its game grades, with 0 as an average Power 4 team. +3 or better is Dawg, −3 or worse is Soft. Big-game and routine averages leave out the setting boost, so a team doesn't look like it rose just because the stage was bigger. With only a game or two, those averages are nudged toward the team's overall number so one game can't put a team at the edge of the chart.

big-stage drop = big-game grade − routine grade

Luck

Wins minus the wins a team's play earned. Each game's win chance comes from the play-by-play with garbage time removed: how often plays succeeded, explosive plays, penalties, turnovers forced and given away, and field-goal tries judged by distance. The parts that are mostly chance (who falls on a fumble, interception swings, missed kicks, return touchdowns) are left out, so they show up as luck. +2 means two more wins than the play earned. Lucky teams tend to come back to earth.
