Red River Rivalry · Week 6

Boomer Sooner!What did we actually learn?

Oklahoma beat No. 1 Texas while getting outgained by 144 yards. Its defense and special teams scored 14 points. Its offense scored 13. That's not a footnote. It's how the Sooners won the Red River Rivalry.

At halftime in the Cotton Bowl, Oklahoma had 69 yards of offense. One of them came on the ground.

No. 1 Texas led 17–7. ESPN's win probability had the Sooners at about 6 percent to start the third quarter. Arch Manning had already thrown two touchdown passes, and the Longhorns' defense had Oklahoma's offense going nowhere.

Then Oklahoma did the thing the box score doesn't capture well. It made the plays that decide close games, and it made almost all of them.

When Tate Sandell's 47-yard field goal went through as time expired, Oklahoma had beaten the No. 1 team in the country, 27–24, in front of 92,100 at the Cotton Bowl. Texas finished with 382 yards to Oklahoma's 238. Texas also finished with two turnovers to Oklahoma's zero, a blocked punt, a missed field goal and its first loss of the season.

The offense scored 13.
The defense and special teams scored 14.

The 14 that didn't come from the offense

The blocked punt. Early in the second quarter, Oklahoma sacked Manning on third-and-12 and pushed Texas back to its own 8. On the punt, Danny Okoye got a hand on it and Dakoda Fields fell on the ball in the end zone. A game Texas had controlled was tied 7–7.

The pick-six. With the game tied 17–17 and 10:07 left, Texas faced second-and-13 at its own 20. Peyton Bowen picked off Manning at the 42 and ran it all the way back. Oklahoma's first lead of the day, 24–17.

Two plays, 14 points, and neither one needed Oklahoma's offense to move the ball an inch.

The 13 that did

Oklahoma's offense didn't need to be great. It needed to be there when the door opened.

Sandell hit from 53 in the third quarter to make it 17–10, the only points of a quarter in which Texas didn't score. Late in the third, a 29-yard Texas punt handed Oklahoma the ball at the Texas 47. That drive should have ended when a trick-play pass from receiver Isaiah Sategna III was intercepted, but roughing the passer wiped it out. A pass-interference flag in the end zone on third-and-9 set up first-and-goal at the 2, and Lloyd Avant punched it in early in the fourth quarter. 17–17.

It was Oklahoma's first offensive touchdown against Texas since 2023. Avant finished with 72 yards on 10 carries.

Texas answered. Manning found J. Bishop Jr. from 15 yards out to tie it at 24 with 6:32 left, and the Longhorns got two more chances. A long field-goal try missed with 1:54 to go. Then, with 24 seconds left and Texas near midfield, Courtland Guillory intercepted Manning at the Oklahoma 39.

John Mateer, who finished 18 of 26 for 155 yards without a turnover, hit Sategna for 13 and then 20. Sandell did the rest.

What Texas did, and what it didn't

Texas fans will look at 382 yards and three Manning touchdown passes and wonder how this got away. Fair. Manning finished 19 of 31 for 261 yards, and the Longhorns' defense held Oklahoma to 69 yards in the first half.

But the game was lost in specific places. The blocked punt came after Texas let a third-down sack push it back to its own 8. Texas went scoreless in the third quarter. A short punt and two penalties gave Oklahoma its only touchdown drive. And both of Manning's interceptions came in the fourth quarter of a tie game. Texas was the better team for long stretches. Oklahoma was the better team in the moments that decided it.

What the DAWG numbers add

DAWG grades every game two ways against what the closing line expected. Texas was a 7.5-point favorite.

Put those two numbers next to each other and the story of the game falls out. On the scoreboard, Oklahoma blew past expectations. Snap for snap, it played about the way a 7.5-point underdog usually does against a team that good. The difference between +11 and +1 is mostly a blocked punt, plus the timing of Texas's mistakes.

That doesn't make the win smaller. Blocking a punt is football. Jumping a route with the game tied is football. It just tells you how Oklahoma won: not by out-gaining Texas, but by winning the plays that swing a game seven points at a time. About one team in five beats its line by 11 or more on a given week. Doing it against the No. 1 team, in this rivalry, is the part Sooner fans will remember.

It also wasn't a quarterback's game. DAWG's QB ratings had both Mateer and Manning below an average Power 4 quarterback for the afternoon.

Haven't we seen this before?

Oklahoma fans might feel a little déjà vu. Last Nov. 15, Oklahoma, then No. 11 in the College Football Playoff rankings, won 23–21 at No. 4 Alabama with 212 yards to Alabama's 406. Alabama turned it over three times, Oklahoma turned those into 17 points, and Eli Bowen returned an interception 87 yards for a touchdown.

Different season, different stage, plenty of new faces. But the shape is familiar: badly outgained, won anyway, with the defense scoring or setting up the points that mattered. Is that who Oklahoma is now, or two great nights 11 months apart? Two games can't settle it. It's a fair argument to have, and November will add evidence.

So what did we actually learn?

For Oklahoma: its defense and special teams were good enough to beat the No. 1 team on a day the offense managed 238 yards. Those were real plays, not luck, but one game can't tell us how often they'll happen again. We also didn't learn whether the offense has turned a corner. One touchdown and two field goals say not yet.

For Texas: this wasn't an exposure. Texas moved the ball, Manning made throws, and the snaps graded close to what the line expected. It was a loss at the margins, on special teams and in the fourth quarter, and a No. 1 team doesn't get to lose those.

The scoreboard says Oklahoma beat No. 1. The play-by-play says how: 14 points from the guys who usually don't score them, and a kicker who didn't miss when it counted.

Boomer Sooner.

What this doesn't tell us

  • One game is one game. Across the Power 4, how a team plays against expectations in one half of a season barely predicts the other half. This result doesn't forecast Oklahoma's or Texas's next game.
  • Season numbers are still small samples. After five games, Oklahoma's season DAWG is −4.7 (52nd of 68 Power 4 teams) and Texas's is −1.7 (40th). Both are well inside what random swings produce over five games.
  • One game's PLAY is noisy. A single game's PLAY is good to about ±12 points. +1 means "about as expected," not "exactly."
  • Rankings. "No. 1" is Texas's AP rank going into the game. This story doesn't use the new poll.
